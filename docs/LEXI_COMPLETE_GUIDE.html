1Quick start — your first five minutes

Open Teams → the Lexi (Hermes) chat. Try these, in order:

  • today — your calendar for the day, in one message.
  • pending — anything waiting on your decision (this is the source of truth — always trust it over old messages in the scroll).
  • inbox review — what's landed in your email in the last 24 hours, triaged.
  • prebrief Jon Kinning — a full who-is-this-person brief: their CRM record, your email history with them, plus fresh web research.
  • Set up a 30-minute intro call with jane@example.com next week — mornings her time, she's in Boston — the full machine: Lexi finds rule-compliant times, drafts the email, holds the slots, and asks you to approve before anything sends.
The one rule to remember: nothing external ever happens without you saying so. Every email send, calendar invite, task write, and CRM note passes through your explicit approval — approve #N — and every decision is recorded with who approved it and when.

2How to talk to Lexi

Plain English works. Lexi is an AI agent with ~90 specialized abilities behind her; you never have to know their names. Say what you want the way you'd tell an assistant:

  • What does my Thursday look like?
  • Who am I meeting tomorrow? Brief me on anyone external.
  • Reply to Natalie — happy hour works, offer her a couple of times late next week.
  • Add a task to IFG Tasks: send the Q3 pre-read, due Friday.
  • What's the latest news on the Denver construction market?
  • Remember: never book me Friday afternoons.

Being specific pays off

When you're scheduling, include your constraints in the same breath — they flow straight into the search: window ("next week", "week of the 17th"), time of day ("mornings", "after 3"), and where the other person is ("she's in Boston", "mornings her time"). "Her time" genuinely means her clock — a Boston "mornings her time" produces 6–10 AM Mountain options, which is 8–noon Eastern.

Session resets

Type /new to start a fresh conversation. Do it when you switch topics or a chat has gone long — it keeps responses fast and cheap. You lose nothing that matters: your saved rules, the pending queue, and everything in the database survive a reset. Only the visible chat context clears.

Timing expectations

  • Simple questions (calendar, pending, lookups): a few seconds.
  • Slot searches and drafts: ~15–60 seconds (she's reading your real calendar).
  • Deep research briefs on a person: up to a minute or two.
  • New email is noticed within ~seconds via a live feed, with a backup sweep every few minutes.

4Scheduling — the full story

This is Lexi's core job, and the part most thoroughly tested. Two ways in:

A. You start it (delegation)

Set up a coffee with Mike Rowland (mike@x.com) — in person, Cherry Creek, whenever I'm back in town

  1. Search. Lexi reads your live calendar and finds 2–3 times that pass all your rules (§5) — honoring your stated window, time-of-day, and the other person's timezone.
  2. Draft + hold. She writes the offer email (in your voice from your mailbox, or as "Lexi, Kory's assistant" from hers — say "as Lexi" to choose) and shows you everything. If nothing fits your ask, she says so honestly: "No availability for next week — offering August 18, 20 and 25 instead." You always know when an offer deviates from what you asked.
  3. You approve. approve #N → the email sends and the offered slots are held on your calendar so nothing double-books them while you wait.
  4. They pick. When the reply lands, Lexi reads which time they chose — including timezone conversion ("noon ET works" maps to the right 10 AM MT slot) — and pings you.
  5. You approve the invite. One more approve #N → the real calendar invite goes out with the Teams link (or venue address for in-person), the unused holds are released, and it's booked.

B. Someone emails you

Lexi watches your inbox continuously. Replies on threads she's handling and things you delegate get acted on and pinged to you. Cold requests from strangers deliberately do NOT interrupt you — they go to your briefings and the review queues instead (Teams pings are for decisions, not FYIs). When you want one handled: schedule the coffee with the guy from the Construction Network email and Lexi picks the thread up from the queue.

Changing your mind — all supported

  • Different time: approve #212 but do Thursday at 3 instead
  • Edit the email: show draft #212 → change it to mention the Q3 numbers, then send
  • They declined the times: Lexi finds fresh options and re-stages for your approval (never re-sends on her own).
  • Reschedule a booked meeting: ask, and the replacement invite fully replaces the old one — the original is only removed after the new time is confirmed.
  • Cancel a booked meeting: cancel meeting #212 — conflict came up

Built-in protections (all verified live)

  • Conflict re-check at booking. The calendar is authoritative at the moment the invite is created, not when times were offered. If something landed on the chosen slot in between, Lexi refuses to book, keeps the holds protecting the slot, and asks you — there is deliberately no override switch.
  • Holds age out. If a recipient never answers, you get a reminder first; expired offers have their holds cleaned off your calendar automatically.
  • Stated durations are honored. A 30-minute ask books 30 minutes — except coffee, which always books 60 (your rule: they run long). A modified time can never produce a zero-length meeting.
  • Every send is yours. Auto-send is off at the system level. No approval, no email — ever.

5Your rules — what Lexi enforces for you

6Email

What she does with your inbox

  • Watches continuously — new mail is triaged (who it's from, what they want, how urgent) within moments of arriving.
  • Surfaces what matters: inbox review for the day's picture, unanswered for real people awaiting your reply, and the review queues for scheduling-shaped threads.
  • Searches on demand: find the thread where Travis mentioned the plumbing deal.

Writing and sending

  • Two voices. "In my voice" = written as you, sent from your mailbox. "As Lexi" = she writes as your assistant from lexi@iconicfounders.com, introduced simply as "Kory's assistant". Scheduling replies default to continuing the existing thread the way a human EA would.
  • Draft-first, always. You can review any email with show draft #N and edit it in plain words before it goes.
  • No approval, no send. There is no path around this.
Note: emailing lexi@iconicfounders.com directly does nothing — nobody's watching that mailbox. Lexi works from your inbox and this Teams chat. To hand her an email thread, CC her on it, forward it, or just mention it here.

7Calendar & daily briefings

  • today / what's my week look like? / am I free Thursday at 2? — reads across your calendars (main, master, birthdays…), timezone-correct, all-day events labeled properly.
  • Availability intelligence — knows the difference between hard blocks (training, Do Not Move, travel) and soft ones; classifies busy time by type when searching.
  • Invites you receive — accept the invite from Sarah / decline the 4pm on Friday.
  • Meeting prep — prebrief lists today's meetings; prebrief <person> or brief me on my 10 o'clock produces the deep version: who they are, your history, their company, recent news.

The morning briefing email

Your daily briefing email (calendar, triaged inbox, tasks, prep) arrives early each morning (~4:30 AM MT). That's produced by the dashboard system, not this chat — but everything in it, you can also ask Lexi about interactively.

8Asana tasks

  • Reads span every project you have — personal board, IFG Tasks, Call List, Marketing Content Calendar, all of it. what's overdue? shows your tasks by default (teammates' work on shared boards is filtered out; say "everyone's" to see all).
  • Create anywhere, by name — add a task to IFG Tasks: send the Q3 pre-read, due Friday. No project named → your personal board. Ambiguous name → she asks rather than guessing.
  • Full management — update, complete, move between sections, comment, delete: mark the cigars task done, move it to YPO, push the due date to Monday.
  • Someone else's task? She'll tell you whose it is and ask you to confirm before touching it — never silently.
  • Reservation reminders — confirmed meal/venue meetings automatically stage a "Needs Reservation" task with the details.
  • Date sanity — "due August 3" always resolves to the next August 3, never a date in the past.

9HubSpot CRM

Reads — ask anything

  • who is Kevin Rabe? — the CRM record: title, company, phone, status, history.
  • what changed in the CRM this week? — new contacts, deal-stage movements.
  • deals snapshot — open pipeline at a glance.
  • CRM health — your book: missing titles/companies/phones, never-contacted counts.
  • Duplicate detection and contact enrichment run in propose-only mode — you see what would change; nothing merges or fills without you.

Writes — deliberately narrow

  • Meeting notes: log a note on Jon Kinning — great call, wants the deck, follow up after Labor Day. Filed on the right record, timestamped.
  • Guardrails proven live: a fuzzy name match is never written to (exact record or nothing); a contact owned by someone else requires your explicit confirmation; Do Not Contact records are excluded from everything outreach-shaped, always.

10Research & the outside world

Lexi reaches the web only when the question needs it — your calendar/email/CRM questions never touch the internet.

  • People — research Jane Doe at Acme before Thursday: web + news + your own email history with them + their CRM record, in one brief.
  • News — latest news on the Denver construction market — dated articles with sources.
  • Places — good coffee shops in Cherry Creek — ratings and addresses.
  • Travel — flights DEN to Houston on the 17th / hotels near the Galleria that week. Research only — she cannot book travel.
  • Anything else — weather, a company, a URL you paste: she'll look it up and give a sourced answer.

11Memory — teaching Lexi your rules

  • Add a rule: Remember: no calls before 9 on Mondays — it immediately shapes every future slot search. This is for durable preferences, not one-off requests.
  • See what she knows: what do you remember about my preferences?
  • Change a rule: just restate it — the new version replaces the old.
  • Remove a rule: forget the rule about Monday calls — she deletes it and quotes back exactly what was removed. If your phrase matches more than one rule she lists them and asks which, deleting nothing until you say.

Separately, Lexi keeps her own operational notes (things she's learned about how you like things done — e.g. that "as Lexi" means her voice from her mailbox). Those make her better over time without any action from you.

12When Lexi asks for help (escalations)

When she can't do something compliantly — no valid times in the window, a lunch/weekend/travel exception, a send failure — she never guesses. She explains the specific blocker and gives you concrete options, always ending with exactly how to respond:

"Coffee mornings are packed the week of the 17th — the earliest compliant options are the following week.
How to respond: reply with guidance (e.g. "try the week after"), or "reject #4242 — reason" to drop it."
  • Reply in plain words — try the week after / offer the 6am slot, she's East Coast — and the search re-runs with your guidance.
  • Or kill it — reject #4242 — not worth pursuing. Works on escalated items too.
  • If you just type "yes" or "ok", she'll confirm which item you mean before doing anything.

You are the only escalation target — Lexi never hands your issues to anyone else, and never claims she has.

14The safety model — why you can trust it

  • Approval gate: every outbound action requires your typed decision. Auto-execute and immediate-send are off in production config.
  • Audit trail: every approval/rejection/modification is recorded — the decision, its source, who authorized it, and when. Every send, hold, booking, and cancellation writes an audit entry.
  • Calendar truth at booking: conflicts are re-checked at the moment the invite is created; holds protect offered times in between.
  • Honest disclosure: if an offer deviates from what you (or the sender) asked — different week, relaxed preference — the approval message says so before you approve.
  • Fails closed: anything uncertain (ambiguous task name, ambiguous rule to delete, fuzzy CRM match, someone else's record, policy conflict) stops and asks rather than guessing.
  • Verified: every capability in this guide was exercised against the live systems on 2026-08-08 — including the complete schedule→offer→accept→book→cancel lifecycle — with an automated test suite of ~680 checks behind it.

15FAQ & troubleshooting

“There's a card with buttons in my chat — should I tap it?”

No. Cards are retired leftovers from pre-launch testing and can even resurface looking new after system restarts. They're harmless (verified), but ignore them. pending is always the truth.

“I approved something but nothing seems to have happened.”

Type pending — if the item is gone, it executed. Lexi also confirms every execution in chat. If something failed, she tells you exactly what and stages a retry for your go-ahead.

“Can I ask her to do several things at once?”

Yes — what's my day, anything pending, and brief me on my 2 o'clock works fine.

“How do I hand her an email I got?”

Mention it (the email from Sarah about the board dinner — offer her some times) — she'll find the thread. CCing her on an email also puts it in front of her.

“What does a proposal number mean? Do I have to use it?”

Every piece of work gets a number (#8021) so you and Lexi can refer to the same thing unambiguously. You never have to know one — describe what you mean ("cancel my coffee with Mike", "approve the intro call with Jane") and Lexi resolves the number herself, asking only if two things could match. Use numbers as shorthand once she's shown them to you.

“The chat is slow or acting confused.”

Type /new for a fresh session. If something seems genuinely broken, tell Anjana — everything is logged, and a timestamp plus what you typed is enough to find it.

“Where does the morning email come from?”

The dashboard system sends it (~4:30 AM MT daily). Lexi is the interactive side; the briefing is the push side. Same data, two doors.
